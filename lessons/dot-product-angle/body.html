Two arrows, one number

The big idea

The dot product takes two arrows and gives back one number that measures how much they point the same way. Divide out their lengths and what's left is the cosine of the angle between them.

Topic 1 · from first principles

The dot product and the angle

What a vector is

A vector is an arrow with a direction and a length. In 3D we write it as three numbers in angle brackets, like \(\langle 1,2,2\rangle\). Those numbers are its components, and you can read them as walking directions.

Start at the origin, the point \((0,0,0)\) where the three axes meet. Walk 1 along \(x\), then 2 along \(y\), then 2 up along \(z\). The arrow runs straight from where you started to where you ended.

We name vectors with bold letters like \(\mathbf a\), and write its components as \(a_1\), \(a_2\), \(a_3\). Your notes may draw a small arrow on top instead, as \(\vec a\). Both mean the same thing.

How long an arrow is

The length of a vector, also called its magnitude, is written \(|\mathbf a|\). You find it with the Pythagorean theorem, which says that in a right triangle the longest side squared equals the other two sides squared, added together.

Walking along the floor makes one right triangle, so the floor distance squared is \(a_1^2 + a_2^2\). Climbing \(a_3\) from there makes a second right triangle standing on the first. Using the theorem twice gives the length in one line.

\[|\mathbf a| = \sqrt{a_1^2 + a_2^2 + a_3^2}\]

The dot product

The dot product is a short recipe. Multiply the matching components, then add the three results.

\[\mathbf a \cdot \mathbf b = a_1 b_1 + a_2 b_2 + a_3 b_3\]

The result is one plain number, called a scalar. It has no direction.

What that number means

Put the two tails at the same point. The angle \(\theta\) (the Greek letter theta) is how far you'd turn one arrow to line it up with the other. It's always between 0° and 180°.

Now drop a straight line from the tip of \(\mathbf b\) onto the line that \(\mathbf a\) lies on, meeting it at a right angle. The part of that line from the tail to where you landed is the shadow of \(\mathbf b\) on \(\mathbf a\). This makes a right triangle, with \(\mathbf b\) as its longest side and the shadow next to the angle.

The cosine of an angle in a right triangle is the side next to the angle divided by the longest side. So the shadow's length is \(|\mathbf b|\cos\theta\). The dot product equals the length of \(\mathbf a\) times the length of that shadow.

\[\mathbf a \cdot \mathbf b = |\mathbf a|\,|\mathbf b|\cos\theta\]
Go deeper: why the recipe and the shadow give the same number

Draw the triangle with sides \(\mathbf a\), \(\mathbf b\), and the third side joining their tips, whose length is \(|\mathbf a - \mathbf b|\). The law of cosines is the Pythagorean theorem with a correction for triangles that have no right angle.

\[|\mathbf a - \mathbf b|^2 = |\mathbf a|^2 + |\mathbf b|^2 - 2|\mathbf a|\,|\mathbf b|\cos\theta\]

Now work out the left side from components, using the length formula.

\[\begin{aligned}|\mathbf a - \mathbf b|^2 &= (a_1-b_1)^2 + (a_2-b_2)^2 + (a_3-b_3)^2\\ &= |\mathbf a|^2 + |\mathbf b|^2 - 2(a_1b_1 + a_2b_2 + a_3b_3)\end{aligned}\]

The two lines describe the same length. Cancel \(|\mathbf a|^2 + |\mathbf b|^2\) and the \(-2\) from both, and the recipe equals \(|\mathbf a|\,|\mathbf b|\cos\theta\).

Analogy · pulling a wagon

Picture a wagon that can only roll straight ahead, with you pulling on its handle. \(\mathbf a\) is the way it rolls, and \(\mathbf b\) is your pull. Pull straight ahead and all of your effort counts. Pull at a slant and only the part along the track counts, which is the shadow. Pull straight up and none of it counts, so the dot product is zero. Lean backward and you're fighting the wagon, so the dot product goes negative.

Definition and angle formula

For \(\mathbf a = \langle a_1,a_2,a_3\rangle\) and \(\mathbf b = \langle b_1,b_2,b_3\rangle\), neither one the zero vector:

\[\mathbf a \cdot \mathbf b = a_1 b_1 + a_2 b_2 + a_3 b_3 = |\mathbf a|\,|\mathbf b|\cos\theta\] \[\cos\theta = \frac{\mathbf a \cdot \mathbf b}{|\mathbf a|\,|\mathbf b|}, \qquad 0^\circ \le \theta \le 180^\circ\]

Dot product, divided by both lengths, gives the cosine of the angle.

Set any two whole-number vectors and every line of the method updates. Try this: leave \(\mathbf a\) alone and change \(\mathbf b\) until the dot product is exactly 0. Then rotate the picture until you can see the right angle.

Watch out. Put your calculator in degree mode before you press \(\cos^{-1}\). In radian mode the same button gives a number like 1.2, which is an angle measured in radians. It's correct, but it isn't in degrees.
Quick check: are \(\langle 1,2,3\rangle\) and \(\langle 3,0,-1\rangle\) perpendicular?

Yes. The dot product is \(3 + 0 - 3 = 0\), so \(\cos\theta = 0\) and \(\theta = 90^\circ\).

Example 1Angle between two vectors

Find the angle between \(\mathbf a = \langle 1,2,2\rangle\) and \(\mathbf b = \langle 2,0,1\rangle\).

  1. Draw the two arrows

    Put both tails at the origin. \(\mathbf a\) walks 1, 2, 2 along \(x\), \(y\), \(z\). \(\mathbf b\) walks 2, 0, 1. The angle \(\theta\) we want sits between them at the origin.

  2. Multiply matching components, then add
    \[\mathbf a \cdot \mathbf b = (1)(2) + (2)(0) + (2)(1) = 2 + 0 + 2 = 4\]

    It's positive, so expect an angle smaller than 90°.

  3. Find the length of \(\mathbf a\)
    \[|\mathbf a| = \sqrt{1^2 + 2^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{9} = 3\]
  4. Find the length of \(\mathbf b\)
    \[|\mathbf b| = \sqrt{2^2 + 0^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{5} \approx 2.236\]
  5. Put the three numbers into the formula
    \[\cos\theta = \frac{\mathbf a \cdot \mathbf b}{|\mathbf a|\,|\mathbf b|} = \frac{4}{3\sqrt5} \approx 0.5963\]
  6. Undo the cosine

    Inverse cosine, written \(\cos^{-1}\) or arccos, answers the question "which angle has this cosine?" Set your calculator to degrees and compute \(\theta = \cos^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{4}{3\sqrt5}\right)\).

Reveal the answer
\(\theta \approx 53.4^\circ\)That's about 0.93 radians. The exact value is \(\cos^{-1}\frac{4}{3\sqrt5}\). It's under 90°, which matches the positive dot product.

Cheat sheet

Dot product
\(\mathbf a \cdot \mathbf b = a_1b_1 + a_2b_2 + a_3b_3\)

Multiply matching components and add. The result is a number.

Length
\(|\mathbf a| = \sqrt{a_1^2 + a_2^2 + a_3^2}\)

The Pythagorean theorem, used twice.

Angle
\(\cos\theta = \dfrac{\mathbf a \cdot \mathbf b}{|\mathbf a|\,|\mathbf b|}\)

Then take \(\cos^{-1}\), in degree mode. The result is from 0° to 180°.

Sign test
\(\mathbf a \cdot \mathbf b = 0\)

Zero means perpendicular. Positive means under 90°. Negative means over 90°.

Common mistakes

  • Dividing by only one length. The formula divides by both.
  • Forgetting the square root, so \(\langle 1,2,2\rangle\) gets a length of 9 instead of 3.
  • Leaving the calculator in radian mode, then writing the result with a degree sign.
  • Stopping at the cosine. \(\cos\theta\) is a ratio, and the question asks for \(\theta\).
  • Measuring the angle with the arrows tip to tail. Both tails must start at the same point.
